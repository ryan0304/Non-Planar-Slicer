# Logo concepts (02-06)

> **Concept 06 (Constructed Trident) is the deployed mark.** It ships inline in
> `viewer/index.html` and is exported to `viewer/brand/trident-mark-*.svg`; see
> that folder's README for the geometry and the measured path lengths. The
> files in *this* folder stay as they are -- they are the record of the choice,
> not the source of what runs. Everything else here is still an alternative,
> including Concepts 07a-c, which exist because 06 can read as a crown.

Alternatives to Concept 01 (the original outward-spiral mark), built for the
user to compare and pick from before anything changed in the running app. Open
[`contact-sheet.html`](./contact-sheet.html) to see all six side by side at
120/48/24/16px against the app's own `--bg`.

Every full/small pair here was generated by
`gen_concepts.py` (kept alongside this README's source discussion, not
checked in -- see the "Geometry" note in each section for the exact formula)
rather than hand-drawn, the same way the deployed mark's own path data is
sampled points, not eyeballed curves.

Colours are baked, matching the deployed mark's convention: `#9aa0a6`
(`--ink-muted`) for the stroke, `#2f6bff` (`--accent`) for at most one
element per concept.

## Concept 02 -- Waveform Monogram

`02-waveform-t-full.svg` / `02-waveform-t-small.svg`

**Idea.** The crossbar of a "T" (for Trident) is not decoration sitting on
top of a letterform -- it *is* the bounded Z-wave the nozzle actually rides,
drawn like an oscilloscope trace, bracketed by two faint dashed guide lines
at the wave's amplitude ceiling. The trace bends into a straight stem,
without lifting, and ends at the nozzle dot.

**Why it suits this product.** It puts the wave-modulated-Z idea front and
center instead of implying it through a spiral silhouette, and the guide
lines are a direct nod to `z_amp_max` being a hard ceiling, not a suggestion.
It also gives the product a legible monogram, which the deployed spiral
deliberately does not.

**Honest weakness.** It reads as "T for Trident" only if you already know the
product name; someone cold has no reason to parse a wavy shape as a letter.
The guide lines, which carry real meaning, are the first thing to disappear
at small sizes -- so the "capped" idea only survives at 48px and up.

**Geometry.**
```
crossbar:  y(x) = ymid + A * sin(2*pi*cycles * (x-x0)/(x1-x0)),  x in [x0,x1]
           x0=16, x1=80, ymid=27, A=6.5, cycles=2.5
guides:    y = ymid +/- (A + 2), dashed, stroke-opacity 0.35
stem:      straight line from the crossbar's transition point to (48, 80)
small:     A single half-cycle (one hump) instead of 2.5 full cycles;
           guide lines dropped entirely.
```

## Concept 03 -- Conformal Contour Rings

`03-contour-drape-full.svg` / `03-contour-drape-small.svg`

**Idea.** Concentric rings at unequal radii, like elevation contours on a
topographic map of a dome, seen from directly above. The spacing is not
decorative: equal steps in height produce contours that bunch together near
the rim (steep slope) and spread apart near the pole (shallow top) -- the
actual visual signature of a shell conforming to a curved surface, which is
one of this software's real printing modes.

**Why it suits this product.** It is the one concept about *conformal
draping* specifically, rather than the spiral-bead claim the deployed mark
and Concepts 02/04/05 all circle back to. It is also deliberately not a
"layer stack" (flat, evenly spaced, parallel lines) -- the thing this
software explicitly does not print -- because the rings are concentric and
unevenly spaced by a real formula, not stacked and uniform.

**Honest weakness.** Out of context, concentric rings read generically as a
"target," "radar," or "sonar" icon before they read as "contour map of a
draped shell." It needs the README/tooltip to carry the specific meaning;
the glyph alone communicates "rings," not "conformal."

**Geometry.** Equal-Z contours of a hemisphere of radius `R`, in plan:
```
r_k = R * sqrt(1 - (k/N)^2),   k = 1 .. N-1
R = 34, N = 6  ->  5 rings, radii approx 33.5, 32.1, 29.4, 25.3, 18.8
```
Note the differences between consecutive radii (1.5, 2.7, 4.1, 6.5) grow
toward the centre -- that widening gap *is* the "shallow near the pole,
steep near the rim" read. Accent dot marks the pole.
Small variant keeps only the outermost ring (k=1) and one inner ring (k=4)
plus the pole dot -- two rings is the minimum that still reads "nested"
rather than "circle."

## Concept 04 -- Isometric Non-Planar Path

`04-isometric-path-full.svg` / `04-isometric-path-small.svg`

**Idea.** Instead of implying "non-planar" through an abstract flat glyph,
this concept draws an actual 3D path in isometric projection: the head
advances at a constant diagonal along one ground axis while Z -- always
vertical and unforeshortened in true isometric projection -- rides a sine
wave on top of that travel.

**Why it suits this product.** It is the only concept that visually commits
to being a 3D diagram rather than a flat mark, matching the CNC-metrology
aesthetic ("precision instrument," not "logo"). The constant diagonal slope
plus the wobble breaking it is a fairly literal restatement of "the tool
head keeps moving while its height rides a wave."

**Honest weakness.** Isometric marks are unforgiving of thin strokes and
small sizes: the diagonal trend and the wobble compete for very few
pixels, and at 16px the shape can read as a scribble rather than "path in
3D" unless the viewer already expects a diagram. It is also the least
symmetric concept, which makes it sit oddly next to five roughly
radially-balanced marks in a row of app icons.

**Geometry.**
```
s(t) = t                              (distance along the ground axis)
Z(t) = A * sin(k*t)                    (the wave), A=8, k=2*pi*2.3/T, T=54
screen_x = s * cos(30deg)
screen_y = s * sin(30deg) - Z
```
Small variant replaces the smooth multi-cycle sine with a 3-point zigzag
(peak, valley, peak) along the same diagonal, straight lines only -- the
continuous wobble is the first thing to go; the diagonal "this is 3D" read
is what has to survive.

## Concept 05 -- Bounded Aperture Wave

`05-aperture-wave-full.svg` / `05-aperture-wave-small.svg`

**Idea.** A fixed ring with the Z-wave running through it left-to-right,
touching the boundary exactly where it is supposed to and nowhere else. The
wave's domain is chosen so it starts and ends precisely on the ring's own
horizontal diameter -- not an eyeballed coincidence, the numbers are set up
so the intersection is exact.

**Why it suits this product.** This is a direct picture of the invariant
that machine ceilings come from the selected `PrinterProfile`, never a
hardcoded constant: the wave is visibly contained by an external boundary
it does not own, rather than free-floating. It is also the most
"instrument" of the six -- a ring is a natural stand-in for a dial, gauge,
or calibrated limit.

**Honest weakness.** Without the README's explanation, a circle with a wave
through it reads as "sound," "radio," or "audio," not "amplitude capped by
a machine profile" -- the specific meaning is not self-evident from the
glyph. It also overlaps thematically with Concept 02's amplitude guide
lines; the two should not both be picked without discussing that redundancy.

**Geometry.**
```
circle: cx=48, cy=48, r=32
wave:   y(x) = cy + A * sin(2*pi*cycles*(x-x0)/(x1-x0)),  x in [x0,x1]
        x0 = cx-r = 16, x1 = cx+r = 80, A=13, cycles=2
```
`x0` and `x1` are exactly the circle's own left/right points, so the path's
open ends sit on the ring by construction. Small variant keeps the ring
(the most robust primitive at tiny sizes) and collapses the wave to one
shallow half-cycle arc instead of two full cycles.

## Concept 06 -- Constructed Trident

`06-constructed-trident-full.svg` / `06-constructed-trident-small.svg`

**Idea.** The product's own namesake, rebuilt honestly rather than avoided:
a three-tine zigzag (tip, valley, tip, valley, tip) drawn as one unbroken
stroke, where every interior corner is replaced with a fixed-radius arc --
exactly the corner-rounding a real G-code planner performs (junction
deviation / arc blending) so the head never has to stop and restart at a
vertex.

**Why it suits this product.** It is the one concept in "geometric /
constructed" territory: hard straight segments and exact circular fillets
instead of organic sine curves, which reads as CNC/drafting rather than
generic branding. It also answers "why not just use a trident" honestly --
by making the *rounding technique itself* the reason it is not a generic
weapon silhouette, tying the obvious literal mark back to the same
single-continuous-path idea every other concept relies on.

**Honest weakness.** It is the most literal of the six (an actual trident
shape) in a product whose selling point is a printing technique, not a
pitchfork; if the goal is to avoid the obvious pun, this concept works
against that goal even though the corner-fillet construction gives it a
technical excuse to exist. The fillets are also small relative to the
glyph and vanish well before 24px, so the "corner blending" idea is
essentially full-size-only.

**Geometry.** Fixed-radius corner fillet applied to a 7-point polyline:
```
points: (22,78) (22,30) (35,58) (48,18) (61,58) (74,30) (74,78)
radius: 4.2

for each interior vertex P with neighbours P_prev, P_next:
  v1 = normalize(P_prev - P);  v2 = normalize(P_next - P)
  angle = acos(v1 . v2)
  dist  = radius / tan(angle/2)          # trim-back distance on each edge
  t1 = P + v1*dist;  t2 = P + v2*dist    # tangent points
  emit: L t1,  A radius radius 0 0 sweep t2
  (sweep from the sign of the cross product of the two travelled edges)
```
This is the standard tangent-arc corner-rounding construction, computed
exactly (not approximated with Bezier curves) so the fillet radius is a
real, checkable number. Small variant drops the fillets entirely (a 4.2
radius is sub-pixel well before 24px) and is the same polyline with sharp
corners and a heavier stroke.

## Choosing

Open `contact-sheet.html`. Everything is judged fairly against the same
background (`#17191b`) at the same four sizes the app actually uses the
mark at (120/48px favor detail; 24/16px is where it lives day to day:
header bar, tab favicon, splash icon). Picking a concept is a separate
deployment step -- swapping the inline copies in `index.html`, re-exporting
the standalone files in `viewer/brand/`, and updating that folder's own
README -- not something this folder does on its own.

Deploying 06 took one step beyond that list, worth knowing before the next
change: `--mark-len` in `style.css` is the glyph's **real path length**, and
every dash figure in the busy animation is a fraction of it. Re-measure with
`getTotalLength()` and update both values, or the header mark's comet and its
progress fill run out of step with the shape they are drawn on.
